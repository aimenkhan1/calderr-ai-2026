Regulatory Pathways for Gene Therapies

In the United States, gene therapies are regulated by the FDA's Center
for Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER) as biologics rather than
small-molecule drugs, following a distinct approval pathway. Products
typically require an Investigational New Drug (IND) application before
human trials, followed by staged Phase 1 (safety), Phase 2 (efficacy
signal), and Phase 3 (confirmatory efficacy) trials before a Biologics
License Application (BLA) can be submitted.

The FDA's Regenerative Medicine Advanced Therapy (RMAT) designation can
accelerate review for therapies addressing serious conditions with
preliminary clinical evidence of substantial improvement over existing
options, offering more frequent FDA interaction and eligibility for
priority review. In the EU, the equivalent accelerated pathway is PRIME
(PRIority MEdicines) designation through the European Medicines Agency.

A distinct regulatory challenge for gene editing therapies specifically
is long-term safety monitoring: because edits are permanent, regulators
typically require extended follow-up periods (often 15 years) to
monitor for delayed adverse effects such as off-target editing
consequences or insertional oncogenesis from viral vector integration,
even after initial approval is granted.
