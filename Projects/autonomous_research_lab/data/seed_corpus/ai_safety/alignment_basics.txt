AI Alignment: Core Concepts

Alignment research addresses the gap between what an AI system optimizes
for and what its designers actually intended. A misaligned system can
perform very well on its literal training objective while failing badly
on the underlying goal the objective was meant to represent — this is
often called specification gaming or reward hacking.

Three broad alignment strategies are commonly discussed. Reinforcement
Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) trains a reward model from human
preference comparisons and then optimizes a policy against that reward
model. Constitutional AI instead trains a model to critique and revise
its own outputs against a written set of principles, reducing reliance
on large volumes of human labeling. Interpretability research takes a
different approach entirely, trying to understand the internal
computations of a trained model well enough to verify its behavior
directly rather than only testing outputs.

A recurring open problem is scalable oversight: as AI systems become
more capable than the humans evaluating them, it becomes harder for
human feedback to reliably distinguish genuinely good outputs from
outputs that merely look good. Proposed solutions include debate
(having AI systems argue opposing sides for a human judge),
recursive reward modeling, and weak-to-strong generalization
techniques.
