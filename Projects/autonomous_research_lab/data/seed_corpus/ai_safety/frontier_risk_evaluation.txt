Frontier Model Risk Evaluation

Capability evaluations attempt to measure whether a model has crossed a
dangerous threshold before it is deployed, rather than after. Common
evaluation categories include: cyberoffense capability (can the model
find and exploit real vulnerabilities autonomously), biological and
chemical weapons uplift (does the model meaningfully lower the barrier
to acquiring dangerous biological or chemical agents compared to
existing internet resources), autonomous replication and adaptation
(can the model acquire resources, copy itself, and persist without
human help), and persuasion/manipulation capability at scale.

A key methodological challenge is that capability evaluations measure
what a model CAN do under deliberate elicitation, which is a different
question from what it WILL do under normal deployment conditions.
Red-teaming exercises, where specialists deliberately try to elicit
harmful behavior, are used to establish an upper bound on capability,
while separate deployment-safety evaluations assess behavior under
realistic usage patterns.

Responsible scaling policies (RSPs) adopted by several frontier labs
define capability thresholds that trigger additional safety
requirements before further scaling is permitted — for example,
requiring stronger cybersecurity protections once a model crosses a
defined bioweapons-uplift threshold. Critics argue self-imposed RSPs
lack external enforcement; proponents argue they establish norms faster
than formal regulation can be written and passed.
