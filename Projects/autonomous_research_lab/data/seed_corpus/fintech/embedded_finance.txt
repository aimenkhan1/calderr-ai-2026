Embedded Finance and Banking-as-a-Service

Embedded finance refers to non-financial companies offering financial
products directly within their own product experience — a ride-share
app offering a debit card, or an e-commerce platform offering point-of-sale
financing — rather than referring customers out to a separate bank or
lender. This is technically enabled by Banking-as-a-Service (BaaS)
providers, which offer API access to core banking functions (account
creation, card issuance, payment processing, lending) that sit on top
of a licensed bank's charter.

The typical BaaS stack has three layers: a chartered bank holding the
actual banking license and regulatory responsibility, a middleware/BaaS
platform handling the technical integration and much of the compliance
tooling, and the non-bank brand-facing company building the customer
product. This layered structure lets non-bank companies launch
financial products in months rather than pursuing their own banking
charter, but it also means regulatory responsibility is distributed
across multiple parties in ways regulators have scrutinized more closely
following several high-profile BaaS platform failures.

Regulatory attention has increasingly focused on whether chartered banks
in these partnerships maintain adequate direct oversight of the
non-bank companies operating under their charter, rather than
delegating compliance monitoring entirely to the middleware layer.
Several enforcement actions have specifically cited inadequate
transaction monitoring and consumer protection oversight in
bank-BaaS-fintech partnership structures as the root failure.
