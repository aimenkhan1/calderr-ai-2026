Real-Time Payment Rails: Architecture Comparison

Real-time payment systems settle transactions in seconds rather than
the multi-day windows typical of legacy ACH (Automated Clearing House)
transfers. In the United States, the FedNow Service (launched by the
Federal Reserve) and The Clearing House's RTP network both provide
real-time settlement between participating banks, using ISO 20022
messaging standards that carry richer structured data than older
payment message formats — enabling more automated reconciliation on the
receiving end.

Internationally, India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) is often cited
as the highest-volume real-time retail payment system, using a
standardized addressing scheme (a "virtual payment address" rather than
a raw bank account number) that lets any participating app interoperate
with any bank, and processing tens of billions of transactions monthly.
Brazil's Pix system, launched by the central bank, achieved similarly
rapid adoption by mandating participation from banks above a certain
size and offering zero-fee person-to-person transfers.

A key architectural distinction is between systems built on a central
bank's own real-time gross settlement (RTGS) infrastructure — where
funds move directly between banks' central bank reserve accounts,
carrying essentially zero counterparty risk — versus deferred net
settlement systems, where a private operator nets transactions
throughout the day and settles the net position periodically, which is
operationally simpler but reintroduces some settlement risk between
settlement windows.
