Stablecoin Design and Reserve Models

Stablecoins aim to maintain a fixed value, typically pegged 1:1 to a
fiat currency, using one of three broad design approaches. Fiat-collateralized
stablecoins hold reserves of cash and cash-equivalent assets (short-term
government securities, commercial paper) in bank accounts or custodial
accounts, redeemable on demand by authorized participants — the model
used by the largest stablecoins by market capitalization. Crypto-collateralized
stablecoins instead lock other cryptocurrencies as collateral, typically
over-collateralized (e.g., $150 of crypto backing $100 of stablecoin) to
absorb price volatility in the underlying collateral asset.

Algorithmic stablecoins attempt to maintain the peg through automated
supply expansion and contraction mechanisms rather than direct
collateral backing, using arbitrage incentives to push the price back
toward the peg. This category has a poor track record — a widely
studied algorithmic stablecoin collapse in 2022 demonstrated how a
loss-of-confidence spiral can overwhelm the arbitrage mechanism once
the peg breaks meaningfully, since the same mechanism that is designed
to restore the peg under normal conditions can accelerate the collapse
once confidence is lost.

Reserve composition and attestation quality have become the central
regulatory focus for fiat-collateralized stablecoins specifically.
Regulators have pushed for reserves to be held in highly liquid,
short-duration assets rather than longer-dated or less liquid
instruments, and for regular independent attestations (and eventually
full audits) of reserve holdings, following incidents where reserve
composition was less liquid or more opaque than users assumed.
